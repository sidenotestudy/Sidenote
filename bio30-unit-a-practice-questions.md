# Bio 30 Unit A: Nervous & Endocrine Systems — Practice Questions
### sidenote practice set — Biology 30

50 original questions matching Biology 30's real diploma exam format: **40 multiple-choice + 10 numerical-response** (Bio 30's actual exam is 48 MC + 12 NR — a roughly 80:20 split, different from Chem 30's ~70:30). 100% machine-scored, no written response.

**A note on the numerical-response questions in this set:** Unit A is anatomy/mechanism-heavy rather than calculation-heavy, so most of its real diploma numerical-response questions aren't arithmetic — they ask you to sequence events in order or count/match structures, which is one of Alberta Education's actual official numerical-response formats (confirmed in the current bulletin). The questions below follow that same real format.

## Part 1: Multiple Choice (40 questions)

**Neuron Structure & Function**

1. Which part of a neuron receives incoming signals from other neurons?
   A. Axon
   B. Dendrites
   C. Axon terminal
   D. Myelin sheath

2. What is the function of the myelin sheath surrounding an axon?
   A. To produce neurotransmitters
   B. To speed up nerve impulse transmission by insulating the axon
   C. To detect stimuli directly
   D. To store neurotransmitters between impulses

3. The gaps between segments of myelin sheath along an axon are called:
   A. Synapses
   B. Dendrites
   C. Nodes of Ranvier
   D. Axon terminals

4. Which type of neuron carries impulses from the central nervous system to an effector, such as a muscle?
   A. Sensory neuron
   B. Motor neuron
   C. Interneuron
   D. Receptor neuron

5. Which type of neuron carries impulses from a receptor toward the central nervous system?
   A. Sensory neuron
   B. Motor neuron
   C. Effector neuron
   D. Interneuron

6. The junction between two neurons, across which a signal is transmitted, is called a:
   A. Node of Ranvier
   B. Synapse
   C. Axon hillock
   D. Dendrite

**Nerve Impulse & Action Potential**

7. In a resting neuron, the inside of the cell membrane is:
   A. More positive than the outside
   B. More negative than the outside
   C. Equal in charge to the outside
   D. Impossible to determine

8. During depolarization of a neuron, which ion rushes into the cell?
   A. Potassium (K⁺)
   B. Sodium (Na⁺)
   C. Chloride (Cl⁻)
   D. Calcium (Ca²⁺), only at the synapse

9. During repolarization, which ion moves out of the cell to restore the resting potential?
   A. Sodium (Na⁺)
   B. Potassium (K⁺)
   C. Chloride (Cl⁻)
   D. Calcium (Ca²⁺)

10. A neuron will only fire an action potential if the stimulus reaches:
    A. Any strength, no matter how small
    B. The threshold potential
    C. The resting potential exactly
    D. Zero millivolts only

11. The "all-or-none" principle of nerve impulses means that:
    A. A neuron fires a partial impulse for weak stimuli
    B. Once threshold is reached, a neuron fires a full-strength action potential, regardless of how much stronger the stimulus is
    C. Neurons never fire twice in a row
    D. Only motor neurons follow this rule

12. Saltatory conduction refers to:
    A. The impulse jumping from node to node along a myelinated axon, increasing speed
    B. The impulse travelling continuously along an unmyelinated axon
    C. The release of neurotransmitter at the synapse
    D. The resting potential of the neuron

13. Immediately after an action potential fires, a brief period exists during which the neuron cannot fire again. This is called the:
    A. Resting potential
    B. Refractory period
    C. Threshold period
    D. Synaptic delay

14. The sodium-potassium pump helps maintain the resting potential by:
    A. Passively allowing ions to diffuse freely across the membrane
    B. Actively transporting Na⁺ out of the cell and K⁺ into the cell, against their concentration gradients
    C. Releasing neurotransmitters at the synapse
    D. Producing myelin

**Synaptic Transmission**

15. When an action potential reaches the axon terminal, what is released into the synaptic cleft?
    A. Sodium ions
    B. Neurotransmitters
    C. Myelin
    D. Hormones directly into the blood

16. Neurotransmitters cross the synaptic cleft and bind to receptors located on the:
    A. Presynaptic membrane
    B. Postsynaptic membrane
    C. Myelin sheath
    D. Node of Ranvier

17. After a neurotransmitter has transmitted its signal, it is typically removed from the synaptic cleft by:
    A. Permanently remaining bound to the receptor
    B. Reuptake into the presynaptic neuron or breakdown by enzymes
    C. Conversion into a hormone
    D. Diffusion into the myelin sheath

18. Which structures within the axon terminal store neurotransmitter molecules before their release?
    A. Mitochondria
    B. Synaptic vesicles
    C. Ribosomes
    D. Nodes of Ranvier

19. What triggers synaptic vesicles to fuse with the presynaptic membrane and release their contents?
    A. The arrival of an action potential at the axon terminal
    B. A drop in blood glucose
    C. The binding of a hormone to the neuron
    D. Myelin degradation

20. Compared to a nervous system signal, an endocrine (hormonal) signal is generally:
    A. Faster acting and shorter in duration
    B. Slower acting and longer lasting
    C. Identical in speed and duration
    D. Only used for reflexes

**CNS/PNS Organization & the Autonomic System**

21. The central nervous system (CNS) is made up of:
    A. All nerves outside the brain and spinal cord
    B. The brain and spinal cord
    C. Only sensory neurons
    D. Only the endocrine glands

22. The peripheral nervous system (PNS) is made up of:
    A. The brain and spinal cord only
    B. All nerves and neurons outside the brain and spinal cord
    C. Only motor neurons
    D. Only the autonomic nervous system

23. The somatic nervous system primarily controls:
    A. Involuntary functions such as heart rate
    B. Voluntary movements of skeletal muscle
    C. Hormone release only
    D. Sensory perception exclusively, with no motor function

24. The autonomic nervous system controls:
    A. Voluntary skeletal muscle movements only
    B. Involuntary functions such as heart rate, digestion, and breathing rate
    C. Only the release of neurotransmitters at the synapse
    D. Only reflex arcs

25. The sympathetic division of the autonomic nervous system is generally associated with:
    A. "Rest and digest" responses
    B. "Fight or flight" responses
    C. Voluntary muscle contraction
    D. Sensory perception only

26. The parasympathetic division of the autonomic nervous system is generally associated with:
    A. "Fight or flight" responses
    B. "Rest and digest" responses
    C. Increasing heart rate during exercise
    D. Releasing adrenaline

**Reflex Arc**

27. A reflex arc allows the body to respond to a stimulus:
    A. Only after conscious thought in the brain
    B. Quickly, often without direct involvement of the brain
    C. Only during sleep
    D. Only through the endocrine system

28. In a simple spinal reflex, the signal typically travels through the spinal cord and:
    A. Always continues to the brain before a response occurs
    B. Can trigger a motor response without first reaching the brain
    C. Never involves a motor neuron
    D. Bypasses the sensory neuron entirely

29. Which neuron in a reflex arc directly connects the sensory neuron to the motor neuron within the spinal cord?
    A. Effector neuron
    B. Interneuron
    C. Receptor neuron
    D. Autonomic neuron

30. What is the first component involved in a reflex arc, responding directly to a stimulus?
    A. Motor neuron
    B. Effector
    C. Receptor
    D. Interneuron

**Endocrine Basics, Hormones & Negative Feedback**

31. Hormones are chemical messengers that are released by:
    A. Neurons only
    B. Endocrine glands, directly into the bloodstream
    C. Muscles only
    D. The synapse only

32. A hormone exerts its effect only on cells that have:
    A. A nucleus
    B. Specific receptors for that hormone
    C. Myelin
    D. An axon

33. The hypothalamus and pituitary gland work together to:
    A. Directly digest food
    B. Control and regulate the release of many other hormones throughout the body
    C. Only control voluntary muscle movement
    D. Produce myelin for neurons

34. A "releasing hormone" from the hypothalamus typically acts on the:
    A. Adrenal cortex directly
    B. Anterior pituitary gland
    C. Thyroid gland directly
    D. Pancreas directly

35. Insulin, released by the pancreas, causes body cells to:
    A. Release more glucose into the blood
    B. Take up glucose from the blood, lowering blood glucose levels
    C. Increase heart rate
    D. Produce more myelin

36. Glucagon, also released by the pancreas, has the opposite effect of insulin by:
    A. Lowering blood glucose further
    B. Stimulating the release of stored glucose, raising blood glucose levels
    C. Directly transmitting nerve impulses
    D. Producing neurotransmitters

37. A negative feedback loop works to:
    A. Amplify a change in the body further and further
    B. Counteract a change and return the body toward a stable set point
    C. Have no effect on body conditions
    D. Only operate during exercise

38. Which of the following is an example of a negative feedback loop maintaining homeostasis?
    A. Blood clotting cascade amplifying rapidly
    B. Insulin and glucagon regulating blood glucose around a stable level
    C. Childbirth contractions increasing in intensity
    D. A snowball rolling downhill and growing larger

39. What is the general relationship between the nervous system and the endocrine system in coordinating body functions?
    A. They operate completely independently and never interact
    B. They often work together, with the hypothalamus acting as a key link between the two systems
    C. The endocrine system controls only reflexes
    D. The nervous system produces all hormones directly

40. Compared to nervous system responses, endocrine responses tend to have effects that are:
    A. Localized to a single cell only
    B. Widespread, affecting many target cells throughout the body
    C. Limited only to reflex arcs
    D. Impossible to reverse

## Part 2: Numerical Response (10 questions)

1. Order the following events in a nerve impulse, from first to last: (1) Repolarization as K⁺ ions exit the cell; (2) Resting potential is restored; (3) Depolarization as Na⁺ ions rush into the cell; (4) Threshold potential is reached. Record your answer as a four-digit number.

2. Order the following steps of synaptic transmission, from first to last: (1) Neurotransmitter binds to receptors on the postsynaptic membrane; (2) An action potential arrives at the axon terminal; (3) Neurotransmitter diffuses across the synaptic cleft; (4) Synaptic vesicles fuse with the presynaptic membrane and release neurotransmitter. Record your answer as a four-digit number.

3. Order the following components of a simple reflex arc, from first to last: (1) Motor neuron; (2) Sensory neuron; (3) Receptor; (4) Effector; (5) Interneuron (in spinal cord). Record your answer as a five-digit number.

4. How many main divisions make up the peripheral nervous system (somatic and autonomic)?

5. How many divisions make up the autonomic nervous system (sympathetic and parasympathetic)?

6. Order the following events in negative feedback regulation of blood glucose after a meal, from first to last: (1) Body cells take up glucose from the blood, lowering blood glucose levels; (2) Blood glucose levels rise after eating; (3) The pancreas detects the rise and releases insulin; (4) Insulin binds to receptors on body cells. Record your answer as a four-digit number.

7. How many neurons, at minimum, are involved in a simple spinal reflex arc that includes an interneuron (sensory, inter, motor)?

8. Order the following structures in the path of a nerve impulse along a myelinated axon, from the cell body to the axon terminal: (1) Axon terminal; (2) Axon hillock; (3) Node of Ranvier; (4) Cell body. Record your answer as a four-digit number.

9. The anterior pituitary releases tropic hormones that directly control three major glands: the thyroid, the adrenal cortex, and the gonads. How many glands are listed?

10. Order the following events describing hypothalamus-pituitary control of hormone release, from first to last: (1) The target endocrine gland releases its hormone into the blood; (2) The hypothalamus releases a releasing hormone; (3) The anterior pituitary releases a tropic hormone; (4) The releasing hormone travels to the anterior pituitary. Record your answer as a four-digit number.
