# Bio 30 Unit C: Cell Division, Genetics & Molecular Biology — Practice Questions
### sidenote practice set — Biology 30

50 original questions — **35 multiple-choice + 15 numerical-response**. This unit uses a different split than Units A and B: Alberta Education's own released items and prep guidance consistently flag genetics (Punnett square ratios, probability, dihybrid crosses) as where the real exam's numerical-response questions concentrate, so this set shifts more toward NR to match. Unit C is also the heaviest-weighted unit on the diploma (35–45%).

## Part 1: Multiple Choice (35 questions)

**Mitosis**

1. The purpose of mitosis is to:
   A. Produce four genetically unique haploid gametes
   B. Produce two genetically identical diploid daughter cells for growth and repair
   C. Introduce genetic variation through crossing over
   D. Reduce the chromosome number by half

2. During which phase of mitosis do chromosomes align along the cell's equator (metaphase plate)?
   A. Prophase
   B. Metaphase
   C. Anaphase
   D. Telophase

3. During anaphase, what happens to the sister chromatids of each chromosome?
   A. They condense and become visible for the first time
   B. They separate and move to opposite poles of the cell
   C. They align at the metaphase plate
   D. The nuclear envelope reforms around them

4. The nuclear envelope breaks down and chromosomes become visible during:
   A. Telophase
   B. Anaphase
   C. Prophase
   D. Interphase

5. Cytokinesis refers to:
   A. The duplication of DNA before division
   B. The physical division of the cytoplasm, forming two separate daughter cells
   C. The alignment of chromosomes at metaphase
   D. The pairing of homologous chromosomes

**Meiosis**

6. Meiosis differs from mitosis primarily in that meiosis:
   A. Produces two genetically identical diploid cells
   B. Produces four genetically varied haploid cells
   C. Occurs only in somatic cells
   D. Does not involve DNA replication

7. Crossing over, which increases genetic variation, occurs during which stage of meiosis?
   A. Prophase I
   B. Metaphase II
   C. Anaphase I
   D. Telophase II

8. Independent assortment refers to:
   A. The random alignment of homologous chromosome pairs during metaphase I, leading to varied combinations in gametes
   B. The exact copying of DNA during interphase
   C. The fusion of sperm and egg
   D. The separation of sister chromatids during meiosis II

9. Homologous chromosomes separate from each other during which stage of meiosis?
   A. Meiosis I
   B. Meiosis II
   C. Both meiosis I and II equally
   D. Neither; only sister chromatids separate in meiosis

10. Sister chromatids separate from each other during which stage of meiosis?
    A. Meiosis I only
    B. Meiosis II
    C. Prophase I
    D. Interphase

11. Which of the following contributes to genetic variation among offspring produced through sexual reproduction?
    A. Crossing over and independent assortment during meiosis, plus random fertilization
    B. Mitosis occurring in somatic cells
    C. DNA replication being perfectly identical every time
    D. Meiosis producing genetically identical cells

**Mendelian Genetics**

12. In a monohybrid cross between two heterozygous individuals (Aa × Aa), what phenotypic ratio is expected in the offspring?
    A. 1:1
    B. 3:1
    C. 9:3:3:1
    D. 1:2:1

13. An organism that is heterozygous for a trait has which genotype?
    A. Two identical alleles (e.g., AA or aa)
    B. Two different alleles (e.g., Aa)
    C. Only one allele present
    D. No alleles for that gene

14. In incomplete dominance, the heterozygous phenotype is:
    A. Identical to one of the homozygous parent phenotypes
    B. A blend or intermediate between the two homozygous phenotypes
    C. Always identical to the recessive phenotype
    D. Impossible to predict

15. In codominance, the heterozygous phenotype shows:
    A. A blended intermediate trait
    B. Both alleles' traits fully and simultaneously expressed
    C. Only the dominant trait
    D. Only the recessive trait

16. A Punnett square is used to:
    A. Sequence DNA
    B. Predict the possible genotype and phenotype combinations of offspring from a genetic cross
    C. Measure population growth
    D. Identify mutations directly

17. A testcross is performed by crossing an individual of unknown genotype with an individual that is:
    A. Heterozygous for the trait
    B. Homozygous dominant for the trait
    C. Homozygous recessive for the trait
    D. Of unknown genotype as well

18. Which best describes a dihybrid cross?
    A. A cross tracking a single trait
    B. A cross tracking two different traits simultaneously
    C. A cross between two identical homozygous individuals
    D. A cross that never produces genetic variation

19. In a dihybrid cross between two double heterozygotes (AaBb × AaBb), assuming independent assortment, the expected phenotypic ratio is:
    A. 3:1
    B. 1:1
    C. 9:3:3:1
    D. 1:2:1

**Sex-Linked Traits & Pedigrees**

20. Sex-linked traits are typically carried on which chromosome in humans?
    A. Any autosome
    B. The X chromosome
    C. The Y chromosome exclusively
    D. Mitochondrial DNA only

21. Why are X-linked recessive traits more commonly expressed in males than females?
    A. Males have two X chromosomes, doubling their chances
    B. Males have only one X chromosome, so a single recessive allele is expressed without a second allele to mask it
    C. Females cannot carry X-linked traits
    D. Males are immune to X-linked conditions

22. A pedigree is a diagram used to:
    A. Show the DNA sequence of an individual
    B. Track the inheritance of a trait through multiple generations of a family
    C. Measure population size
    D. Show the stages of mitosis

23. In a pedigree, a filled-in (shaded) symbol typically represents an individual who:
    A. Is unaffected by the trait being studied
    B. Displays the trait or condition being studied
    C. Is deceased
    D. Cannot be analyzed

24. A mother who is a carrier for an X-linked recessive condition (unaffected herself) has which genotype?
    A. Homozygous recessive
    B. Heterozygous, with one normal allele and one recessive allele on her X chromosomes
    C. Homozygous dominant only
    D. She cannot be a carrier if unaffected

**DNA Structure & Replication**

25. The building blocks of DNA are called:
    A. Amino acids
    B. Nucleotides
    C. Codons
    D. Ribosomes

26. In DNA, adenine always pairs with:
    A. Guanine
    B. Cytosine
    C. Thymine
    D. Uracil

27. DNA replication is described as "semi-conservative" because:
    A. Each new DNA molecule is made entirely of new nucleotides
    B. Each new DNA molecule consists of one original (parental) strand and one newly synthesized strand
    C. DNA is destroyed and rebuilt from scratch each time
    D. Only half of the DNA molecule is ever replicated

28. The enzyme primarily responsible for unwinding and separating the two strands of the DNA double helix during replication is:
    A. DNA polymerase
    B. Helicase
    C. Ribosomal RNA
    D. Ligase

29. The overall shape of a DNA molecule is best described as a:
    A. Single straight chain
    B. Double helix
    C. Branched tree structure
    D. Circular loop only

**Transcription & Translation**

30. Transcription is the process by which:
    A. A protein is assembled from amino acids
    B. An mRNA molecule is synthesized from a DNA template
    C. DNA is copied into more DNA
    D. Amino acids are linked into a polypeptide directly from DNA

31. In RNA, which base replaces thymine, which is found in DNA?
    A. Adenine
    B. Guanine
    C. Cytosine
    D. Uracil

32. Translation is the process by which:
    A. DNA is copied into mRNA
    B. mRNA is decoded by a ribosome to build a polypeptide chain, with tRNA supplying amino acids
    C. Chromosomes condense before mitosis
    D. Homologous chromosomes pair up

33. A codon is best described as:
    A. A single nucleotide base
    B. A sequence of three nucleotide bases on mRNA that codes for a specific amino acid
    C. An entire gene
    D. A type of ribosome

34. tRNA molecules function by:
    A. Storing genetic information permanently
    B. Carrying specific amino acids to the ribosome and matching them to the correct codon via their anticodon
    C. Directly copying DNA
    D. Breaking down proteins after synthesis

35. A mutation that changes a single nucleotide base in a gene's sequence could potentially result in:
    A. No change to the protein at all, in every case
    B. A change in the amino acid sequence of the resulting protein, potentially altering its function
    C. Immediate cell death in every case
    D. Conversion of the gene into RNA directly

## Part 2: Numerical Response (15 questions)

1. A cross between two heterozygous parents (Aa × Aa) for a simple dominant/recessive trait produces offspring in a phenotypic ratio of dominant to recessive individuals. Record this ratio as a two-digit number (e.g., a 1:1 ratio would be recorded as 11).

2. A dihybrid cross between two individuals heterozygous for two independently assorting genes (AaBb × AaBb) produces offspring in a phenotypic ratio. Record this ratio as a four-digit number representing dominant-dominant : dominant-recessive : recessive-dominant : recessive-recessive (e.g., 9:3:3:1 would be recorded as 9331).

3. In a monohybrid cross between two heterozygous parents (Aa × Aa), what fraction of offspring are expected to be homozygous recessive (aa)? Record your answer as a decimal, to two decimal places.

4. How many chromosomes are present in a human somatic (body) cell during metaphase of mitosis, before division is complete?

5. How many chromosomes end up in each of the two daughter cells produced by a single mitotic division of a human somatic cell (starting with 46 chromosomes)?

6. How many daughter cells are produced from a single cell that undergoes one complete round of meiosis?

7. How many chromosomes are present in each daughter cell produced by meiosis of a human cell that started with 46 chromosomes?

8. A pedigree shows a recessive X-linked trait. An unaffected, heterozygous (carrier) mother and an unaffected father have children. What fraction of their sons are expected to be affected by this X-linked recessive trait? Record your answer as a decimal, to two decimal places.

9. In DNA replication, if one strand of a DNA molecule has the base sequence 5'-ATCGGT-3', how many nucleotide bases are in its complementary strand?

10. During transcription, an mRNA codon consists of how many nucleotide bases?

11. A gene's coding region contains 300 nucleotide bases. How many amino acids would this sequence code for in the resulting protein, assuming every three bases code for one amino acid?

12. How many possible different codons can be formed from combinations of the four DNA/RNA nitrogenous bases, taken three at a time?

13. Order the following steps of protein synthesis, from first to last: (1) Ribosome translates mRNA into a polypeptide chain; (2) mRNA is transcribed from a DNA template in the nucleus; (3) mRNA exits the nucleus and travels to a ribosome; (4) tRNA molecules bring specific amino acids to the ribosome according to the codon sequence. Record your answer as a four-digit number.

14. Order the following phases of mitosis, from first to last: (1) Anaphase; (2) Metaphase; (3) Prophase; (4) Telophase. Record your answer as a four-digit number.

15. In a testcross between an individual of unknown genotype for a dominant trait and a homozygous recessive individual, the offspring show a 1:1 ratio of dominant to recessive phenotypes. This indicates the unknown parent was most likely which genotype? Record 1 for homozygous dominant, or 2 for heterozygous.
