## Part 1: Multiple Choice (38 questions)

**Population Growth Models**

1. Exponential population growth occurs when:
   A. Resources are limited and competition is high
   B. Resources are abundant and few limiting factors are present
   C. The population has reached carrying capacity
   D. Death rate exceeds birth rate

2. A logistic growth curve differs from an exponential growth curve in that logistic growth:
   A. Increases indefinitely without limit
   B. Levels off as the population approaches the carrying capacity of the environment
   C. Always decreases over time
   D. Never involves limiting factors

3. Carrying capacity refers to:
   A. The maximum population size an environment can sustainably support
   B. The minimum population size needed for survival
   C. The total number of species in an ecosystem
   D. The rate at which a population reproduces

4. Which of the following is a density-dependent limiting factor?
   A. A sudden temperature drop affecting all individuals equally regardless of population size
   B. Competition for food, which intensifies as population density increases
   C. A natural disaster affecting the whole population equally
   D. Seasonal changes unrelated to population size

5. Which of the following is a density-independent limiting factor?
   A. Disease spreading more easily in crowded populations
   B. Competition for mates
   C. A wildfire or natural disaster affecting individuals regardless of population density
   D. Predation increasing with prey density

6. On a logistic growth curve, population growth rate is typically highest:
   A. At the very start of the curve, when population size is smallest
   B. At an intermediate population size, before the population approaches carrying capacity
   C. Exactly at carrying capacity
   D. Growth rate is constant throughout

7. A population that has grown beyond its carrying capacity will typically experience:
   A. Continued unlimited growth
   B. A population crash or decline as resources become insufficient
   C. No change at all
   D. Immediate extinction with no decline first

8. Which factor directly increases population size?
   A. Emigration
   B. Mortality
   C. Natality (births)
   D. Predation

**Natality, Mortality, Migration & Limiting Factors**

9. Which of the following combinations of factors would increase a population's size?
   A. High natality and high immigration
   B. High mortality and high emigration
   C. Low natality and high mortality
   D. High emigration and low immigration

10. Emigration refers to:
    A. Individuals entering a population from elsewhere
    B. Individuals leaving a population to live elsewhere
    C. Individuals being born into a population
    D. Individuals dying within a population

11. Immigration refers to:
    A. Individuals leaving a population
    B. Individuals entering a population from elsewhere
    C. The birth rate of a population
    D. The death rate of a population

12. A population's growth rate can be calculated using which combination of factors?
    A. Natality and mortality only, ignoring migration
    B. Natality, mortality, immigration, and emigration together
    C. Only immigration and emigration
    D. Only carrying capacity

13. Limiting factors are best described as:
    A. Factors that have no effect on population size
    B. Factors that restrict the growth of a population
    C. Factors that only apply to plant populations
    D. Factors that guarantee unlimited population growth

14. Which of these is an example of a density-dependent limiting factor?
    A. A volcanic eruption
    B. An unusually cold winter affecting all individuals equally
    C. The spread of disease, which increases with population density
    D. A random flood

**Community Interactions**

15. Predation is best described as an interaction in which:
    A. Two species compete for the same limited resource
    B. One organism (the predator) hunts and consumes another organism (the prey)
    C. Two species live together with no effect on either
    D. One organism benefits while causing no harm or benefit to another

16. Competition between two species for the same limited resource is called:
    A. Predation
    B. Interspecific competition
    C. Mutualism
    D. Parasitism

17. In a mutualistic relationship, the two species involved:
    A. Both benefit from the interaction
    B. One benefits while the other is harmed
    C. One benefits while the other is unaffected
    D. Both are harmed

18. In a commensalistic relationship:
    A. Both species benefit equally
    B. One species benefits while the other is neither helped nor harmed
    C. One species is harmed while the other benefits
    D. Both species are harmed

19. In a parasitic relationship:
    A. Both species benefit
    B. One species (the parasite) benefits while the other (the host) is harmed
    C. Neither species is affected
    D. The host benefits more than the parasite

20. Ecological succession refers to:
    A. The sudden extinction of a species
    B. The gradual, predictable change in a community's species composition over time
    C. The random and unpredictable disappearance of an ecosystem
    D. The migration of a single species between habitats

21. Primary succession begins in an area:
    A. That previously had a developed community, disturbed by fire or logging
    B. With no pre-existing soil or life, such as bare rock after a volcanic eruption
    C. That already contains a mature climax community
    D. Only in aquatic ecosystems

22. A climax community represents:
    A. The very first stage of succession
    B. A relatively stable, mature community that persists until a major disturbance occurs
    C. A community with no interactions between species
    D. A community that always eventually disappears entirely

**Hardy-Weinberg & Population Genetics**

23. The Hardy-Weinberg principle describes a population in which:
    A. Allele frequencies change rapidly generation to generation
    B. Allele and genotype frequencies remain constant from generation to generation, assuming certain conditions are met
    C. Only dominant alleles are passed on
    D. Mutation rates are always high

24. In the Hardy-Weinberg equation p² + 2pq + q² = 1, the term "2pq" represents:
    A. The frequency of homozygous dominant individuals
    B. The frequency of heterozygous individuals
    C. The frequency of homozygous recessive individuals
    D. The total population size

25. In the Hardy-Weinberg equation, p typically represents:
    A. The frequency of the recessive allele
    B. The frequency of the dominant allele
    C. The total number of alleles in the population
    D. The frequency of heterozygotes

26. Which of the following would violate the assumptions of Hardy-Weinberg equilibrium?
    A. A very large population size with no mutation
    B. Random mating throughout the population
    C. Natural selection favoring certain genotypes over others
    D. No migration into or out of the population

27. For a population to remain in Hardy-Weinberg equilibrium, which condition must generally be met?
    A. Small population size with significant genetic drift
    B. No mutation, no migration, no natural selection, random mating, and a large population size
    C. Strong natural selection pressure
    D. Frequent migration between populations

28. A gene pool refers to:
    A. A single individual's complete set of genes
    B. The total collection of genes and alleles present in a population
    C. Only the dominant alleles in a population
    D. A pool of water where genetic material is stored

29. Genetic drift is best described as:
    A. A directional change in allele frequencies due to random chance, especially significant in small populations
    B. A change in allele frequencies due to natural selection only
    C. A process that only occurs in large populations
    D. The same process as natural selection

30. The Hardy-Weinberg principle is often used as a baseline to:
    A. Prove that evolution never occurs
    B. Detect whether evolution (a change in allele frequencies) is occurring in a real population, by comparing it to this theoretical non-evolving baseline
    C. Calculate population growth rates directly
    D. Measure carrying capacity

**Natural Selection, Evolution & Gene Pools**

31. Natural selection acts on:
    A. Genotypes directly, regardless of any observable effect
    B. Phenotypes, which are the observable expression of an individual's genotype interacting with the environment
    C. Only recessive alleles
    D. Only dominant alleles

32. An individual with a trait that increases its chances of survival and reproduction in a given environment is said to have:
    A. Lower fitness
    B. Higher fitness
    C. No fitness
    D. Fitness unrelated to reproduction

33. Which of the following is a requirement for natural selection to occur within a population?
    A. All individuals must be genetically identical
    B. There must be heritable variation in traits among individuals
    C. The environment must never change
    D. Mutation must be completely absent

34. Over many generations, natural selection can lead to:
    A. No change in a population's allele frequencies
    B. A gradual change in a population's allele frequencies, potentially leading to evolution
    C. The complete elimination of genetic variation
    D. Identical phenotypes across all individuals

35. Which of the following is a source of new genetic variation within a population?
    A. Random mating only
    B. Mutation, which introduces new alleles into a gene pool
    C. Genetic drift only
    D. Natural selection itself

36. Which best describes evolution at the population level?
    A. A change in the physical traits of a single individual during its lifetime
    B. A change in allele frequencies within a population's gene pool over successive generations
    C. The birth and death of individual organisms
    D. A change in an individual's genotype after birth

37. Stabilizing selection favors:
    A. Extreme phenotypes at both ends of a range
    B. Intermediate phenotypes, reducing variation over time
    C. Only the least common phenotype
    D. Random phenotypes with no pattern

38. Directional selection favors:
    A. Intermediate phenotypes
    B. One extreme phenotype, shifting the population's average trait over time
    C. Two separate extreme phenotypes simultaneously
    D. No particular phenotype

## Part 2: Numerical Response (12 questions)

1. In a population at Hardy-Weinberg equilibrium, the frequency of the dominant allele (p) is 0.60. What is the frequency of the recessive allele (q)? Record to two decimal places.

2. Using the allele frequencies from Question 1 (p = 0.60, q = 0.40), calculate the expected frequency of homozygous dominant individuals (p²) in the population, to two decimal places.

3. Using the same allele frequencies (p = 0.60, q = 0.40), calculate the expected frequency of heterozygous individuals (2pq) in the population, to two decimal places.

4. Using the same allele frequencies (p = 0.60, q = 0.40), calculate the expected frequency of homozygous recessive individuals (q²) in the population, to two decimal places.

5. In a population of 500 individuals, 20 individuals show a homozygous recessive phenotype. Calculate q² (the frequency of the homozygous recessive genotype), to two decimal places.

6. Using the q² value calculated in Question 5, calculate q (the frequency of the recessive allele), to two decimal places.

7. Using the q value from Question 6, calculate p (the frequency of the dominant allele), to two decimal places.

8. A population of bacteria grows exponentially, doubling every hour. If the population starts at 100 individuals, what will the population size be after 3 hours?

9. A population has a birth rate of 30 individuals per 1000 per year and a death rate of 12 individuals per 1000 per year, with no migration. Calculate the population growth rate, in individuals per 1000 per year.

10. A population of 2000 individuals has an annual growth rate of 5%. Approximately how many individuals would be added to the population in one year? Record to the nearest whole number.

11. Order the following stages of ecological succession, from first (earliest) to last (most mature): (1) Climax community; (2) Pioneer species colonize bare ground; (3) Shrubs and small trees establish; (4) Grasses and small plants establish. Record your answer as a four-digit number.

12. A population is at carrying capacity. If the environment's carrying capacity is 5000 individuals and the current population is 4850, how many more individuals could the environment theoretically support before reaching carrying capacity?
