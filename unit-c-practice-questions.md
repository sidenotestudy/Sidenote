# Unit C: Chemical Changes of Organic Compounds — Practice Questions
### sidenote practice set — Chem 30

50 original questions, same format as Units A and B: **35 multiple-choice + 15 numerical-response**. Unit C is more pattern-recognition based than the other units (naming, structure, isomers), so the numerical-response questions lean on isomer counts, IUPAC locants, and esterification/polymer stoichiometry rather than pure arithmetic — but they're built the same way: original, matching real diploma weighting (18–20%) and style.

## Part 1: Multiple Choice (35 questions)

**Naming Hydrocarbons**

1. What is the IUPAC suffix used for a hydrocarbon containing only single bonds?
   A. -ene
   B. -yne
   C. -ane
   D. -ol

2. In naming a branched hydrocarbon, the parent chain is chosen as:
   A. The chain drawn horizontally in the structure
   B. The longest continuous carbon chain, regardless of how it is drawn
   C. The chain with the most branches
   D. The shortest chain containing a functional group

3. A hydrocarbon contains one carbon-carbon double bond. Which suffix is used in its IUPAC name?
   A. -ane
   B. -ene
   C. -yne
   D. -oic acid

4. When numbering a carbon chain that contains both a double bond and a branch, which is given priority for the lowest locant?
   A. The branch
   B. The double or triple bond
   C. Alphabetical order of branch names
   D. The end of the chain closest to the largest branch

5. What is the correct IUPAC name for a five-carbon chain, all single bonds, with a one-carbon branch on carbon 2?
   A. 2-methylpentane
   B. 3-methylpentane
   C. 2-methylbutane
   D. pentane

6. Which naming skill does the diploma test in both directions — given a structure and given a name?
   A. Determining boiling point only
   B. Converting between a structure and its IUPAC name
   C. Calculating molar mass only
   D. Identifying the state of matter

**Functional Groups**

7. A carbon-oxygen double bond located at the end of a carbon chain, with a hydrogen also attached to that carbon, indicates which functional group?
   A. Ketone
   B. Aldehyde
   C. Carboxylic acid
   D. Ester

8. A functional group with an O-H group attached directly to a carbon chain is called a(n):
   A. Ester
   B. Alcohol
   C. Amine
   D. Ketone

9. Which functional group is identified by a carbon-oxygen double bond located in the middle of a carbon chain (not at the end)?
   A. Aldehyde
   B. Ketone
   C. Carboxylic acid
   D. Ester

10. A carboxylic acid and an ester have very similar structures. What is the key structural difference between them?
    A. Esters contain nitrogen; carboxylic acids do not
    B. In an ester, the O-H hydrogen of the carboxylic acid is replaced by a carbon chain
    C. Carboxylic acids have no oxygen atoms
    D. Esters always contain a triple bond

11. Which naming ending corresponds to an alcohol?
    A. -oic acid
    B. -one
    C. -ol
    D. -al

12. Which functional group contains a nitrogen atom attached to a carbon chain?
    A. Amine
    B. Ketone
    C. Ester
    D. Alcohol

**Isomers**

13. Two compounds have the same molecular formula but different structural arrangements of atoms. These compounds are called:
    A. Allotropes
    B. Isotopes
    C. Structural isomers
    D. Polymers

14. Which molecular formula could represent two different structural isomers, straight-chain and branched?
    A. CH₄
    B. C₂H₆
    C. C₄H₁₀
    D. C₂H₄

15. Structural isomers of the same molecular formula often differ in:
    A. Molecular formula
    B. Number of atoms of each element
    C. Physical properties such as boiling point
    D. Total mass

16. What type of isomer is explicitly outside the scope of the Chem 30 curriculum?
    A. Structural isomers
    B. Stereoisomers
    C. Chain isomers
    D. Positional isomers

17. To determine whether two given structures are isomers of each other, a student should:
    A. Compare their colours
    B. Count and compare the total number of each type of atom in both structures
    C. Compare their names alphabetically
    D. Assume they are isomers if they both contain carbon

**Addition, Substitution & Elimination**

18. A reaction in which atoms are added directly across a carbon-carbon double bond, converting it to a single bond, is called:
    A. Substitution
    B. Addition
    C. Elimination
    D. Saponification

19. A reaction in which one atom or group on a molecule is replaced by a different atom or group, with the number of pieces roughly unchanged, is called:
    A. Addition
    B. Elimination
    C. Substitution
    D. Esterification

20. A reaction in which a saturated molecule loses atoms to form a new double bond is called:
    A. Addition
    B. Elimination
    C. Substitution
    D. Polymerization

21. Propene reacts with HBr, and the H and Br atoms add across the double bond. This is an example of:
    A. Substitution
    B. Elimination
    C. Addition
    D. Saponification

22. Which question should a student ask to quickly identify whether a reaction is addition or elimination?
    A. Did the molecule gain or lose a functional group?
    B. Did a double bond appear or disappear?
    C. Did the molecular formula stay the same?
    D. Did the reaction release heat?

23. Elimination reactions typically produce:
    A. One product only, always identical to the reactant
    B. A main organic product plus a small byproduct such as water or a hydrogen halide
    C. No products at all
    D. Only carbon dioxide and water

**Esterification & Saponification**

24. Esterification is the reaction between:
    A. An alcohol and a base
    B. A carboxylic acid and an alcohol
    C. Two carboxylic acids
    D. An ester and water only

25. The products of an esterification reaction are:
    A. An ester and water
    B. A carboxylic acid and an alcohol
    C. Two esters
    D. A ketone and an aldehyde

26. Saponification is best described as:
    A. An ester reacting with an alcohol to form a carboxylic acid
    B. An ester reacting with a strong base to form a carboxylic acid salt (soap) and an alcohol
    C. A carboxylic acid reacting with water
    D. An alcohol reacting with another alcohol

27. The product commonly known as "soap" is chemically a:
    A. Carboxylic acid
    B. Carboxylic acid salt
    C. Alcohol
    D. Ester

28. Which memory hook connects saponification to its product?
    A. Saponification always produces an ester
    B. Saponification makes soap
    C. Saponification only occurs in acidic conditions
    D. Saponification requires no base

29. Given a set of reactants, a diploma question may ask a student to:
    A. Predict the products of esterification or saponification
    B. Calculate the boiling point of the product
    C. Identify the reaction as either addition or elimination
    D. Determine the isotope of the product

**Polymers**

30. In addition polymerization, monomers link together:
    A. Releasing a small molecule such as water with every new bond
    B. With nothing left over — no byproduct is released
    C. Only in the presence of a strong base
    D. By losing all their double bonds without forming new bonds

31. In condensation polymerization, a small molecule is released:
    A. Never
    B. Only at the very end of the reaction
    C. Every time a new bond forms between monomers
    D. Only if the monomer contains nitrogen

32. Which type of polymerization does esterification most closely resemble, repeated over and over?
    A. Addition polymerization
    B. Condensation polymerization
    C. Neither
    D. Both equally

33. Most common plastics, such as plastic bags and bottles, are produced using:
    A. Condensation polymerization
    B. Addition polymerization
    C. Saponification
    D. Combustion

**Mixed / Applied**

34. A structure contains a carbon-oxygen double bond and an O-H group on the same carbon. What functional group is this, and what is its IUPAC suffix?
    A. Ester, "-oate"
    B. Carboxylic acid, "-oic acid"
    C. Ketone, "-one"

35. A student is given two structures with the same molecular formula, C₅H₁₂O, but different arrangements of atoms. What must be true about these two structures?
    A. They must be the same compound
    B. They are structural isomers of each other
    C. They cannot both exist
    D. They must have identical boiling points

## Part 2: Numerical Response (15 questions)

1. How many carbon atoms are in the longest continuous chain (parent chain) of the compound named 3-ethyl-2,4-dimethylhexane?

2. In the IUPAC name 4-methylhex-2-ene, at which carbon number does the double bond begin?

3. How many carbon atoms total are present in the molecule 2,2-dimethylpropane (parent chain plus branches)?

4. A hydrocarbon has the IUPAC name 3-methylpent-1-yne. How many carbon atoms are in its parent chain?

5. How many structural isomers exist for the molecular formula C₄H₁₀?

6. How many structural isomers (excluding stereoisomers) exist for the molecular formula C₅H₁₂?

7. Structure X has the molecular formula C₆H₁₄, and Structure Y has the molecular formula C₆H₁₂. How many hydrogen atoms would need to be added to Structure Y for it to become an isomer of Structure X?

8. What mass of ethyl acetate (molar mass = 88.11 g/mol) is produced from the complete esterification of 0.250 mol of acetic acid with excess ethanol, given the reaction proceeds 1:1? Record to three significant figures.

9. If 0.400 mol of a carboxylic acid reacts completely with excess alcohol in an esterification reaction with a 1:1 ratio, how many moles of water are produced as a byproduct?

10. A saponification reaction breaks down 0.150 mol of an ester (1:1 with base) into a carboxylic acid salt and an alcohol. How many moles of alcohol are produced?

11. An esterification reaction has a theoretical yield of 15.0 g of product, but only 12.6 g is actually collected. Calculate the percent yield, to three significant figures.

12. A student needs to produce 0.600 mol of an ester via esterification, with the reaction proceeding at a 1:1 mole ratio of carboxylic acid to ester. How many moles of the carboxylic acid reactant are required, assuming 100% yield?

13. A polyethylene chain has a total molar mass of 28 000 g/mol, built from ethylene monomers (C₂H₄, molar mass = 28.05 g/mol). Approximately how many monomer units make up this polymer chain? Record to the nearest whole number.

14. A condensation polymer forms from 500 monomer units, releasing one water molecule (molar mass 18.02 g/mol) for each new bond formed between adjacent monomers. If 499 bonds form, calculate the total mass of water released, in grams, to three significant figures.

15. A polymer chain contains 250 repeating monomer units, each with a molar mass of 104.15 g/mol (styrene). Calculate the total molar mass of the polymer chain, in g/mol, to three significant figures.
