# Unit D: Chemical Equilibrium Focusing on Acid-Base Systems — Practice Questions
### sidenote practice set — Chem 30

50 original questions, same format as Units A, B, and C: **35 multiple-choice + 15 numerical-response**. Unit D is tied for the heaviest unit on the diploma (29–32%), and it's the most calculation-heavy — ICE tables, Ksp, pH/pOH, and buffers all show up as real numerical-response questions here. Note: the Henderson-Hasselbalch equation is outside Chem 30's scope, so the buffer questions here use the same ICE-table logic as any weak acid problem, not that shortcut formula.

## Part 1: Multiple Choice (35 questions)

**Le Chatelier's Principle**

1. According to Le Chatelier's principle, adding more reactant to a system at equilibrium causes the system to shift:
   A. Left, toward reactants
   B. Right, toward products
   C. Not at all
   D. Toward whichever side has fewer moles of gas

2. Which stress, when applied to a system at equilibrium, changes the actual value of K?
   A. Adding a catalyst
   B. Increasing pressure
   C. Changing temperature
   D. Adding more reactant

3. Adding a catalyst to a system at equilibrium:
   A. Shifts the equilibrium toward products
   B. Shifts the equilibrium toward reactants
   C. Has no effect on the position of equilibrium, only the rate of reaching it
   D. Changes the value of K

4. For a gas-phase reaction at equilibrium, decreasing the volume of the container (increasing pressure) shifts the equilibrium toward:
   A. The side with more moles of gas
   B. The side with fewer moles of gas
   C. Neither side
   D. The side with the highest temperature

5. Increasing the temperature of an endothermic reaction at equilibrium shifts the reaction:
   A. Toward reactants
   B. Toward products
   C. Not at all
   D. In a direction that cannot be determined

**ICE Tables**

6. What does each letter in "ICE table" represent?
   A. Initial, Change, Equilibrium
   B. Ion, Concentration, Equation
   C. Initial, Concentration, Equilibrium
   D. Ion, Change, Energy

7. In an ICE table, the "Change" row typically uses which variable to represent the unknown amount reacting?
   A. K
   B. x
   C. Q
   D. pH

8. In a typical ICE table where reactants form products, the sign convention is:
   A. Reactants gain (+x), products lose (−x)
   B. Reactants lose (−x), products gain (+x)
   C. Both reactants and products gain (+x)
   D. Both reactants and products lose (−x)

9. The values in the "Equilibrium" row of an ICE table are used directly in:
   A. The percent yield calculation
   B. The equilibrium constant expression
   C. The Ksp of an unrelated compound
   D. The pH formula only

10. If solving an ICE table problem produces a quadratic equation, this is often a sign that a student should first check:
    A. Whether the reaction is exothermic
    B. Whether a simplifying approximation is valid instead
    C. Whether the temperature was given in Celsius
    D. Whether K is greater than 1

**Ksp & the Common Ion Effect**

11. Ksp is best described as:
    A. The rate constant for any reaction
    B. An equilibrium constant for a sparingly soluble compound dissolving into its ions
    C. The pH of a saturated solution
    D. A constant that changes with concentration

12. If the calculated value of Q is greater than Ksp for a given set of ion concentrations, this indicates:
    A. The solution is unsaturated and more solid can dissolve
    B. A precipitate will form
    C. The solution is at equilibrium exactly
    D. No further chemistry occurs

13. The common ion effect describes what happens when:
    A. A solution already contains one of the ions from a slightly soluble compound, reducing how much of the solid can dissolve
    B. Two completely different ions are mixed together
    C. A catalyst is added to a saturated solution
    D. Temperature is increased

14. Which principle explains why the common ion effect reduces solubility?
    A. Hess's Law
    B. Le Chatelier's principle
    C. Faraday's law
    D. The ideal gas law

15. If Q equals Ksp exactly for a solution, this indicates the solution is:
    A. Unsaturated
    B. Saturated and at equilibrium, with no net precipitation or dissolving
    C. Definitely going to precipitate
    D. Definitely going to dissolve more solid

**Strong vs. Weak Acids**

16. A strong acid in aqueous solution:
    A. Partially dissociates, requiring an ICE table
    B. Dissociates essentially completely, with [H⁺] equal to the acid's concentration for a monoprotic acid

17. A weak acid in aqueous solution:
    A. Dissociates completely
    B. Only partially dissociates, governed by an equilibrium constant Ka

    D. Cannot be described using an ICE table

18. Which of the following is generally classified as a strong acid?
    A. Acetic acid
    B. Hydrochloric acid
    C. A generic weak organic acid
    D. Carbonic acid

19. Two solutions have the same initial concentration: one is a strong acid, the other is a weak acid. Which statement is correct?
    A. Both will have identical pH
    B. The strong acid solution will have a lower pH than the weak acid solution
    C. The weak acid solution will have a lower pH than the strong acid solution

20. Before starting a calculation, a student should first determine whether an acid is strong or weak because:
    A. It changes which method (direct calculation vs. ICE table) should be used

**Buffers**

## Part 2: Numerical Response (15 questions)

1. A weak acid HA has an initial concentration of 0.150 M and a Ka of 2.0 × 10⁻⁵. Using the approximation that x is small compared to 0.150, calculate [H⁺] at equilibrium, in mol/L, to two significant figures.

2. Using the [H⁺] calculated in Question 1, calculate the pH of the solution, to two decimal places.

3. A weak acid has Ka = 1.0 × 10⁻⁴ and an initial concentration of 0.0800 M. Using the small-x approximation, calculate the equilibrium [H⁺], in mol/L, to two significant figures.

4. A 0.250 M solution of a weak acid has [H⁺] = 3.2 × 10⁻³ M at equilibrium. Calculate the percent dissociation of the acid, to two significant figures.

5. The Ksp of AgCl is 1.8 × 10⁻¹⁰. Calculate the molar solubility of AgCl in pure water, in mol/L, to two significant figures.

6. Equal volumes of 0.0020 M AgNO₃ and 0.0020 M NaCl are mixed. After mixing, calculate Q for AgCl (Ksp = 1.8 × 10⁻¹⁰), given that mixing halves both concentrations. Record to two significant figures.

7. Using the Q value from Question 6 and Ksp = 1.8 × 10⁻¹⁰ for AgCl, will a precipitate form? Record 1 for yes or 0 for no.

8. A solution has [OH⁻] = 4.0 × 10⁻⁴ M. Calculate the pOH of this solution, to two decimal places.

9. Using the pOH from Question 8, calculate the pH of the solution, to two decimal places.

10. A solution has a pH of 5.20. Calculate [H⁺], in mol/L, to two significant figures.

11. Calculate the pH of a 0.045 M solution of HCl (a strong, monoprotic acid), to two decimal places.

12. Calculate the pH of a 0.0125 M solution of HNO₃ (a strong, monoprotic acid), to two decimal places.

13. A buffer is prepared with 0.200 mol/L of a weak acid HA (Ka = 1.8 × 10⁻⁵) and 0.200 mol/L of its conjugate base A⁻, in the same solution. Since the initial concentrations of acid and conjugate base are equal, [H⁺] at equilibrium is approximately equal to Ka. Calculate the pH of this buffer, to two decimal places.

14. A different buffer has equal initial concentrations of a weak acid (Ka = 6.3 × 10⁻⁵) and its conjugate base. Calculate the pH of this buffer, to two decimal places.

15. In a titration, 25.00 mL of a 0.100 mol/L NaOH solution is required to completely neutralize 20.00 mL of an HCl solution of unknown concentration. Calculate the concentration of the HCl solution, in mol/L, to three significant figures.
